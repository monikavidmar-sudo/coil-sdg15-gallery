01 / INTRODUCTION

What are the SDGs?

The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) are 17 global goals adopted by United Nations member states in 2015 as part of the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development. They address interconnected challenges including poverty, inequality, climate change and environmental protection.

01

Understanding SDG 15

SDG 15, “Life on Land,” focuses on protecting, restoring and sustainably using terrestrial ecosystems, managing forests, combating desertification, reversing land degradation and halting biodiversity loss.

02

Why it matters

Forests, healthy soils and diverse species support food production, clean water, climate regulation and human well-being. Damage to these ecosystems affects both wildlife and communities.

02 / LOCAL PERSPECTIVE

SDG 15 in Slovenia

Slovenia is known for its extensive forests, diverse landscapes and rich biodiversity. Protecting these natural resources requires responsible management and long-term conservation.

01

Forests and wildlife

Forests cover more than half of Slovenia. They provide habitats for species such as brown bears, wolves and lynx, while supporting recreation, forestry and ecosystem services.

02

Protected natural areas

Triglav National Park and Natura 2000 sites help conserve important habitats and species. Conservation also depends on how land is used outside protected areas.

03

Environmental pressures

Habitat fragmentation, invasive species, pollution, climate change and extreme weather can threaten ecosystems. Forests also face challenges from drought, storms and pests.

04

What Slovenia can do

Sustainable forestry, habitat restoration, wildlife corridors, careful spatial planning and monitoring of endangered species can help preserve biodiversity.

03 / GLOBAL PERSPECTIVE

SDG 15 Around the World

Healthy terrestrial ecosystems are essential everywhere, but the threats they face vary by region. Global cooperation is needed because biodiversity loss and land degradation cross national borders.

01

Deforestation

Forest clearing for agriculture, infrastructure and other land uses destroys habitats, releases stored carbon and disrupts ecosystems.

02

Biodiversity loss

Habitat destruction, overexploitation, invasive species, pollution and climate change put many plant and animal species at risk.

03

Land degradation

Unsustainable farming, erosion, drought and poor land management can reduce soil fertility and damage livelihoods.

Global challenges require local action, strong policies and international cooperation.

04 / TAKING ACTION

Solutions and Actions

Progress towards SDG 15 depends on governments, scientists, businesses, communities and individuals working together to protect and restore nature.

01

Protect and restore habitats

Conserve forests and wetlands, restore damaged ecosystems and reconnect fragmented habitats. Use native species where restoration is appropriate.

02

Use land sustainably

Promote responsible forestry and agriculture, protect soil, reduce erosion and plan development to limit damage to natural areas.

03

Reduce pollution and waste

Prevent littering, dispose of harmful waste correctly, reduce unnecessary consumption and support circular use of materials.

04

Support education and policy

Learn about local ecosystems, support evidence-based conservation, take part in community initiatives and encourage biodiversity-friendly decisions.

Protecting life on land is not one single action. It is a long-term commitment to healthier ecosystems.
